# Evaluation and modeling roadmap for public-data pre-snap coverage prediction

## What can research tell us about attainable pre-snap performance?

### Takeaway
Pre-snap prediction of eventual seven-class coverage is distinct from identifying coverage after players reveal their assignments. Public research supports testing motion-response features and structured player interactions, but does not establish an attainable seven-class accuracy target for this repository.

### Cited Findings
- The NFL/AWS coverage-classification description explicitly uses interactions before and after the snap and describes ambiguity among coverage categories, including adjustments that complicate both charting and classification. Its success is not a pre-snap benchmark. — [NFL/AWS technical account](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/identifying-defense-coverage-schemes-in-nfls-next-gen-stats/)
- A 2026 preprint predicts binary man/zone from pre-snap motion with HMM-derived defender/receiver assignment features. It studies BDB2025 and a motion-only cohort of 3,963 plays excluding bunch/two-quarterback formations. Main evaluation uses repeated random fivefold cross-fitting; a team analysis uses leave-one-team-out evaluation. The authors report useful incremental motion features, not a seven-class result. Code is linked. Its introduction says 2024 and data section says 2023, but the parallel acquisition audit identifies BDB2025 as 2022 data already present locally. **Do not use the paper's season statements as acquisition evidence or assume it provides a new season.** — [Michels et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10784v1), [author repository](https://github.com/janoleko/BDB-2025/); cross-check the acquisition researcher's official data-source findings.
- A 2026 NFL transformer paper trains on 2020–23 and tests 2024 for individual assignments/matchups. Receiver-defender matchup accuracy is about 75% through the snap versus 89.4% through pass arrival. Those are per-defender matchup outcomes, not seven-class team coverage. The paper describes end-of-play labels conflicting with valid intermediate assignments and ambiguous schemes. Its data includes NFL tracking and PFF labels; publication does not establish public availability of the entire training corpus. — [Song et al., especially §§3–5 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25901v1)
- Earlier unsupervised work clusters defensive-back behavior into man/zone with soft assignments; qualitative interpretation maps clusters to semantic classes. Unsupervised clustering is therefore not itself a source of authoritative seven-class labels. — [Dutta et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11373)

### Inferences
- Proposed modeling order: (1) same-cohort baselines and diagnosis, (2) observable situation and receiver/defender geometry, (3) simple motion-response summaries, (4) a small structured temporal model, (5) only then privileged-teacher or pretraining experiments. This order tests cheaper explanations before expanding architecture or search.
- First motion features: receiver lateral motion, defender following correlation with causal lags, relative separation changes, assignment switches, safety depth/rotation, and defense-wide response to motion. Compare only on matched plays and available history; assess motion and non-motion separately. Fit any learned assignment/HMM model within each training fold. Truncate its input at the exact evaluation cutoff: a smoother using observations through the snap would leak into an earlier cutoff.
- Keep seven-class prediction as the primary goal. Add a binary head (Man: Cover-0/Cover-1/2-Man; Zone: others) as auxiliary supervision and an explicit fallback. A three-class *coverage-family* view could distinguish man / Cover-2–Quarters–Cover-6 zone family / Cover-3, with frozen mapping and honest names. Do not call this observed safety shell, and do not present easier-task improvement as seven-class success. A separately derived pre-snap shell is a feature/task, not the eventual coverage label.
- A hierarchical/multitask loss could preserve binary information while learning fine classes, but this is a hypothesis requiring a matched ablation. Reweighting rare classes can trade precision for recall; show both rather than optimize recall alone.

### Gaps
- No verified public primary source establishes a Bayes ceiling or expected seven-class improvement for this exact cohort and cutoff. The current result cannot diagnose irreducible ambiguity by itself.
- We cannot assume an extra season contains enough distinct rare-class labels until deduplication, schema mapping, and usable-prefix auditing complete.

## How should additional data, learning curves, and labels be evaluated?

### Takeaway
New public data should first buy an honest future-game test and then additional training diversity. Model disagreement can identify suspicious labels autonomously, but cannot certify corrections without independent evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Standard evaluation guidance warns that repeated model selection on test results leaks test knowledge; preprocessing must also be learned on training data. Group-aware and time-aware splits address dependence that ordinary random folds ignore. — [scikit-learn cross-validation guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
- Learning curves evaluate training and validation performance at varying training sizes; the API supports grouped validation and timing. Default row-level subsampling still needs deliberate adaptation to this dataset's games/plays. — [official learning-curve documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.learning_curve.html)
- Confident Learning identifies probable label issues using probabilities and class-conditional noise assumptions. This is useful screening, not proof that an individual football annotation is wrong; semantic ambiguity and feature-dependent disguise do not automatically satisfy its assumptions. — [Northcutt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00068)
- Wilson proportion intervals account for uncertainty from finite binary counts; small denominators produce broad intervals. Their ordinary binomial formulation does not account for football game clustering. — [NIST confidence-interval guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm)

### Inferences
- Proposed protocol: keep the original frozen artifacts untouched as historical evidence. Treat already-inspected 2021/22 evaluation games as exposed development/diagnostic material for a new protocol; never relabel them as fresh. Before scoring any newly acquired cohort, write a manifest separating earlier training/development games, a calibration block, and the latest whole-game holdout. Prefer an untouched later season; if only one new partial season exists, reserve its last available contiguous weeks, subject to predeclared support checks. Do not choose the split by favorable model results.
- A score-blind audit may inspect schemas, joins, exclusions, and label/support counts to decide whether the proposed holdout is evaluable. Log any support-based split amendment before predictions. Preserve all versions. If adequate fresh labeled data cannot be found, report internal developmental improvement only; autonomous execution should produce an explicit unmet-evidence result rather than manufacture a fresh test.
- **Acquisition contingency:** no fresh full-22 labeled pre-snap tracking cohort has been verified by the parallel acquisition researcher. Available newer participation labels alone do not supply missing tracking. Do not create a fictional 2023 holdout from BDB2025: that competition's data is already present as 2022. Until a compatible new cohort is verified, perform diagnostics/learning curves on declared exposed development data and explicitly leave fresh generalization acceptance unmet. If a genuinely new nine-week cohort later qualifies, one possible predeclared allocation is Weeks 1–6 for rolling development/refit, Week 7 calibration, Weeks 8–9 sealed testing, contingent on score-blind class/game support. Exact dates and IDs must follow the verified manifest, not competition-year names.
- All frames/cutoffs/augmentations of a play belong to one split; all plays of a game remain together. Freeze source IDs, chronological availability, hashes and overlap detection across competition editions. No final-game unlabeled pretraining either: make the intended evaluation inductive, not transductive.
- Use three forward chronological validation windows for development, with training-only feature normalization, model fitting, calibration/threshold choices on separate earlier data, and no model choice from fresh holdout scores. Add held-out defensive-team or team-season stress tests as secondary robustness analysis; they answer a different question than future games of known teams. Compute team tendency features from strictly earlier games, with smoothing for unseen teams.
- Proposed learning curve: nested 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% subsets of eligible training **games**, three seeded selections, equal training recipe and sufficient optimization convergence. Plot train/validation macro precision, recall, F1, NLL and per-class recall against unique games and labeled plays. Keep validation fixed and show class counts/history-length mix. Rising validation with sample size suggests additional compatible data is promising; low train and validation performance suggests underfitting/features/labels; high train and stalled validation suggests regularization/distribution/ambiguity. These are diagnostic indications, not proofs.
- Separate quantity from domain: compare old-only versus old+new at matched sizes, then full combined data, and report by season/source. Compare simple and neural models on exactly the same eligible plays, features, cutoff, and split. A source shift can outweigh more examples. Augmented frames and repeated cutoffs do not count as new labeled plays.
- Autonomous label audit: conflicting labels for identical play IDs, impossible vocabulary mappings, inconsistent directions/units, event offsets, duplicate player/time rows, join mismatches and invalid player counts can be caught deterministically. Record original and normalized labels with source provenance. Use out-of-fold predictions from diverse models to rank high-loss disagreements, clustered by source/class/team. Do not auto-relabel a play merely because models agree; compare a conservative downweighting sensitivity run with the unchanged-label baseline. Do not remove difficult test examples after looking at errors.
- Statistical support is not simply “at least 20 per class.” Show true-class count for recall, predicted-positive count for precision, number of contributing games and a game-cluster interval. Proposed all-class acceptance requires each class span at least 10 test games and its recall interval width be no more than 20 percentage points; otherwise mark class-level evidence insufficient. These are proposed precision requirements, not known attainable counts. For orientation, 100 independent examples at 50% recall produce approximately ±10 percentage points, whereas game dependence can widen uncertainty. Never claim “rare classes fixed” from a handful of correct predictions.

### Gaps
- Existing evidence alone cannot determine whether rare-class failure is training scarcity, separability, annotation differences, or optimization. Required counts and a matched learning-curve experiment have not yet been measured here.
- Under a no-human-in-loop constraint, semantic charting disputes remain unresolved unless independently corroborated labels are available. A model can flag them, not establish football ground truth.

## How can an autonomous experiment campaign improve results without leakage or goal drift?

### Takeaway
Use bounded, staged ablations and keep training-only privileged information separate from inference. Establish an absolute usefulness target as well as relative gains, with a fail-closed result when data or statistical support is insufficient.

### Cited Findings
- Learning with privileged information allows information present during training but unavailable at deployment to guide a student. Time-series privileged-information research analyzes when future observations can improve sample efficiency of models using baseline inputs at prediction time; benefits are conditional, not automatic. — [Vapnik and Izmailov](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v16/vapnik15b.html), [Karlsson et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/k-a-karlsson22a.html)
- Temperature scaling is a simple post-hoc probability-calibration method; it does not itself supply missing discriminative information or change the argmax class with positive scalar temperature. — [Guo et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)

### Inferences
- Stage A: recover same-protocol old binary, seven-class tree and neural baselines; fit training-only majority and context-only baselines; quantify rare classes, source shifts, missing history, and calibration. Deliver a reproducible diagnostic report before extra architecture.
- Stage B: learning curves plus three bounded feature bundles: observable situation/context, richer relative geometry/roles/orientation, and causal motion-response. Ablate each independently with the strongest simple baseline. Prefer variables known at prediction time; avoid eventual pass outcome, route, targeted defender, post-snap coverage charting, or post-cutoff tracking as inputs. If the reported population is eventual pass plays, explicitly describe performance as conditional on that retrospectively selected population.
- Stage C: carry only development winners into one small temporal/interaction model and one hierarchical/binary auxiliary objective. Class weights and loss hyperparameters belong to training/development only. Use the same seeds, examples, early-stopping budget, and explicit training-time budget for fair comparisons. Stop new architecture when simpler models dominate.
- Stage D, conditional: if lawful additional tracking lacks trusted coverage labels, test masked-player/trajectory reconstruction pretraining on training-game prefixes, then supervised fine-tuning. This creates representation training examples, not new ground-truth labels. Compare with a randomly initialized model at matched supervised data and report extra compute. Do not assume generic tracking objectives encode coverage.
- Optional privileged teacher: train a post-snap coverage/assignment teacher only from training games, generate soft targets through game-level cross-fitting, and distill into a pre-snap-only student. Keep post-snap data outside inference schemas; use a test that modifies/removes every future frame and confirms student predictions are unchanged. Evaluate teacher/student separately. A teacher-student gap is a diagnostic signal, not a formal upper bound on achievable pre-snap accuracy. Cross-fit pseudo-labels and retain their provenance; never use them as final-test truth.
- Proposed finite campaign: allow at most 12 distinct model/feature/loss configurations after diagnostics, prune on development only, and run three seeds for the two finalists. Check incremental GPU cost before privileged/pretraining stages. One final holdout unlock after all choices, thresholds, and artifact hashes are frozen. Repeated failed holdout revisions become a new exposed-development cycle requiring another future cohort for fresh evidence.
- Proposed minimum experimental advancement: paired game-bootstrap 95% lower bound of macro-F1 improvement above zero versus strongest same-protocol baseline; at least +3 percentage points mean macro-F1 on development; no more than 1 point loss in derived binary F1 or one-second F1; no severe per-class recall regression. This is an improvement gate, not product acceptance.
- Proposed meaningful-gain criterion for the fresh comparison: at least +3 percentage points macro-F1 over the strongest frozen same-cohort baseline and paired game-bootstrap 95% lower bound above zero; report macro precision and recall separately and require neither to regress. Preserve the binary/one-second non-regression gates. Require the rare-class recall improvement claim itself to have positive paired game-bootstrap lower bound, no collapse in rare-class precision, and the support/interval-width requirements above; otherwise state that rare-class improvement is unresolved. No absolute accuracy promise is supported by the research. An eventual product-utility threshold should be a separate declared decision rather than equating this relative-gain gate with reliability.
- For confidence-filtered output, freeze threshold on calibration only and compare answered accuracy at fixed 25%, 50%, and 75% answer rates, with game intervals. Report answer rate/correctness per class and team, all-play macro metrics, NLL/Brier, and calibration plots. Select any operational accuracy target before final scoring based on the intended use; 80% or any other round number has no special validity here. Do not disguise abstention as improved all-play recall, or promise nominal guarantees under year-to-year shift.
- Final autonomous delivery should contain signed/frozen split and source manifests, exclusion and label audits, learning curves, ablation table, seed variation plus game intervals, seven-class precision/recall/confusions, binary/coarse fallback results clearly separate, confidence/answer-rate tradeoffs, and CPU/GPU end-to-end time/memory on identical workloads. Distinguish operational code completion, internally improved model, and fresh-data acceptance.

### Gaps
- No study cited here guarantees a meaningful gain or product utility. Acquisition and diagnostics may show that the public-data seven-class task remains below the desired quality.
- Human-readable coverage concepts and labels may disagree even after technical data checks. Without new independent labels, uncertainty must remain visible rather than “fixed” by automated relabeling.
