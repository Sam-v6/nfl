# Improving pre snap coverage

**Additional public data is worth pursuing, but we have not verified a new, compatible set of fully labeled pre-snap tracking plays.** The strongest leads are older 2018 tracking joined to newly verified public coverage labels, and a schema audit of the newly launched Big Data Bowl 2027 release. Neither is a ready-made training expansion. Meanwhile, the existing experiment has testable weaknesses: scarce rare-class examples, training that repeatedly peaked at its epoch limit, unequal weighting of plays, and limited situation and motion features. The recommended plan combines a small acquisition audit with controlled model diagnosis, then expands only when measured gains justify it. This is a proposal for implementation after approval: public/free sources only, no promised accuracy uplift, and an initial ceiling of **six GPU fits or 30 GPU minutes**.

## Public sources offer leads, not a confirmed new tracking cohort

Competition years must not be confused with football seasons. **BDB 2025 supplies 2022 Weeks 1–9; BDB 2023 supplies 2021 Weeks 1–8. Both are already represented locally.** Reacquiring them could repair missing records, but cannot be counted as new seasons. BDB 2024 substantially overlaps the same 2022 games. ([BDB 2025 dictionary](https://www.kaggle.com/c/nfl-big-data-bowl-2025/data), [BDB 2023 host announcement](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/nfl-big-data-bowl-2023/discussion/359079))

| Public source | Verified opportunity | Condition before using it |
|---|---|---|
| nflverse 2018 participation + BDB 2021 tracking | The participation file contains **18,297 seven-class label candidates**, including 463 2-Man labels. BDB 2021 covers 2018 passing plays. | Tracking excludes offensive and defensive linemen. Pre-snap duration and join yield remain unverified. These are label candidates, **not 18,297 usable new examples**. |
| BDB 2027 | Official announcement includes regular-season tracking alongside Combine tracking. | Audit actual seasons, players, event timestamps, labels and a small sample before bulk acquisition. No suitable new cohort is confirmed. |
| nflverse participation and play-by-play | Public 2023 and 2025 participation files contain 22,916 and 22,055 coverage-labeled rows respectively; useful for a context-only model. | Labels alone do not provide player trajectories. Measure source changes and establish predictor availability at each cutoff. |
| BDB 2026 Analytics | Newer-season movement data; worth a low-priority boundary check. | “Before the throw” does not establish “before the snap.” Do not use it as pre-snap training data without timestamp evidence. |

The participation counts were computed directly from three public Parquet files, with hashes recorded in the research notes. They precede tracking joins, exclusions and cross-source deduplication. The 2018 missing-linemen restriction is documented in the competition input description. ([Official participation release](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/pbp_participation), [2018 source file](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/download/pbp_participation/pbp_participation_2018.parquet), [BDB 2021 description](https://www.kaggle.com/zachdrapkin/target-agnostic-evaluation-of-cbs-wrs/data))

BDB 2027 is a priority because a compatible later season could supply both training diversity and an honest future-game test. Its release description is insufficient to establish that opportunity. BDB 2026 likewise requires direct event inspection; its dictionary includes future information that must never enter pre-snap predictors. ([BDB 2027 data](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/nfl-big-data-bowl-2027/data), [BDB 2026 dictionary](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/nfl-big-data-bowl-2026-analytics/data))

The acquisition audit should record official source, terms, hash, keys, incremental games and plays, available history at each cutoff, missing players, label mapping and join exclusions. Use official access and preserve attribution; free availability does not authorize unrestricted redistribution. Participation changes from NGS through 2022 to FTN afterward, so measure source disagreement rather than silently pooling labels. Map Cover4 to Quarters explicitly; keep unsupported categories outside the seven-class cohort. If lawful access requires unavailable credentials or unaccepted terms, document the blocked source and continue the existing-data experiments. ([Loader and attribution](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_participation.html))

## Existing evidence points to actionable training weaknesses

The frozen result remains **50.51% macro precision, 45.74% macro recall and 46.61% macro-F1**, averaged over three neural seeds. The preselected seed achieved only 4.35% 2-Man recall and 19.27% Cover-6 recall. Those results support further investigation, not reliable seven-class deployment. Preserve the original report and artifacts unchanged. ([Frozen results](../reports/2026-10-11-coverage-comparison/README.md))

There are 11,793 unique training plays, but only **254 2-Man plays**, including just **75 in 2022**. Temporal experiments restricted to 2022 therefore had especially little rare-class supervision. Repeated endpoints and mirrored examples increase training rows without adding independent coverage labels. Moreover, 2022 represents 35.4% of unique training plays but 57.3% of original endpoint rows. Recent-season emphasis could be useful; it is an experimental choice to measure, not automatically a defect. ([Dataset evidence](supporting/diagnosis.md))

Several models reached their best development result at the final allowed epoch, 30. First test a longer, early-stopped schedule and play-balanced sampling while recording optimizer updates, unique plays and elapsed time. A longer run is justified as a convergence diagnostic, not assumed to improve generalization. Keep FP32, matched evaluation precision and fixed cohorts; earlier temporal comparisons mixed precision and history availability. ([Training receipts and diagnosis](supporting/diagnosis.md))

The selected model sees position, velocity and a defense indicator from the last observed frame. It omits down, distance, score, clock, roles and explicit receiver–defender relationships. The temporal alternative pools players before recurrence, which can lose individual trajectories. Test observable context and simple player-level motion summaries before a larger network. A recent pre-snap motion study supports this hypothesis, but its binary, motion-only evaluation is not a seven-class benchmark or a new dataset. ([Model diagnosis](supporting/diagnosis.md), [Motion research](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10784v1))

Automated checks should catch duplicate IDs, inconsistent coordinates, snap offsets, invalid joins and taxonomy conflicts. Out-of-fold model disagreements can prioritize suspicious annotations. **They cannot establish football ground truth without independent labels or expert film review.** Do not automatically relabel hard plays or remove difficult evaluation examples. ([Label-issue detection research](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00068))

## A staged campaign answers one question at a time

Before training, create a separate v2 manifest and ledger. Keep whole games together, including every cutoff and augmentation. Use two chronological outer development windows with earlier, separate inner checkpoint-selection windows; preprocessing also fits within each training fold. Reuse the existing corrected binary references and tree baseline, refitting when the new fold protocol requires it. Existing 2021/2022 evaluation weeks are exposed historical evidence, not a fresh test. ([Cross-validation guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html))

| Ordered work package | Deliverable and dependency | Success or futility decision; GPU cap |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Inventory and protocol | Source/overlap/label/history audit; frozen development IDs; sample-only source qualification. No training dependency. | Acquire more only after usable pre-snap frames, valid joins and permitted access are demonstrated. No GPU. |
| 2. Convergence and sampling pilot | Matched two-fold comparison: current schedule, longer schedule, longer schedule with play-balanced weights. Depends on protocol. | Keep only consistent improvement without binary or one-second deterioration; if flat, move to information diagnosis. **6 fits / 30 minutes maximum**, first batch total. |
| 3. Learning curves | Nested 25/50/100% training-game subsets on both folds, one fixed sampling seed, same recipe and validation IDs; matched CPU LightGBM curves. Depends on pilot. | Rising validation supports acquisition; flat curves redirect effort. These are screening curves, not seed-confirmed conclusions. **6 fits**. |
| 4. Targeted screens | Up to four two-fold arms: causal context/geometry; motion; one objective change; qualified additional data. Depends on audit and curves. | Retain an arm only with ≥2-point mean macro-F1 gain and non-regression guards. Stop inferior arms; do not fill unused slots with arbitrary searches. **8 fits**. |
| 5. Confirmation | At most two recipes, including the retained reference, on two folds with three seeds; paired game-level comparisons. Depends on screening. | Advance only with the stricter gates below; otherwise report a negative result. **12 fits**. |
| 6. Frozen evaluation and delivery | Calibration, permitted final refits, one sealed-test evaluation if available, documentation and performance receipts. Depends on confirmation. | No fresh cohort means development evidence only. **4 reserved GPU executions** for predeclared refits only: for example, three candidate seeds plus one recovery slot. The tabular baseline uses CPU; another three-seed neural-reference refit is not budgeted. |

No acquisition or training is authorized by this document itself; the packages describe work to perform after approval. The full proposal stops at **36 GPU executions or four GPU hours**, whichever comes first. All attempted fits, failed runs, retries and final refits count; startup time counts. This is a ceiling, not a spending target. Use one GPU process, an exclusive project lock and an idle/headroom check; defer work while rendering competes and never interrupt another process. Cap CPU work at four threads and two hours per package, report incomplete checks, and stop a package when its cap expires. Initial acquisition is metadata plus one small sample per candidate source; inspect official file sizes and available storage before any larger transfer.

Context features must exist at the prediction cutoff: down, distance, score, clock and observed ball/line-of-scrimmage-relative geometry are candidates. Final at-snap formation, motion flags or box counts can leak information into a one-second forecast; derive them from the observed prefix or omit them. Give both LightGBM and the neural comparator the same permitted context, training plays and evaluation cohorts. If enriched LightGBM wins, retain it; the goal is predictive quality, not a neural winner. Newer free labels can support a separately evaluated context-only model even without matching tracking. Do not confuse the public FTN charting subset with participation coverage labels. ([Participation dictionary](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_participation.html), [FTN dictionary](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_ftn_charting.html))

For motion, first compare causal displacement, separation changes and defender-following summaries on identical 2022 plays with sufficient history. Only if useful, substitute a small per-player temporal encoder before spatial pooling, preserving within-play identity and elapsed-time masks. For imbalance, test one mild weighting or binary-auxiliary objective; simple inverse-frequency weighting already performed worse. Keep seven-class outputs and separately report binary fallback performance.

If 2018 qualifies, compare current-only and added-data models using the same common-player representation, horizons and validation games. Add a matched-size comparison to distinguish more data from source effects. Otherwise use that screen for a justified representation ablation or leave it unused. Learning curves indicate whether compatible data is promising; they cannot prove that coverage is identifiable before the snap. Post-snap teachers and self-supervised pretraining remain later options, outside this initial campaign.

## Improvement must survive precision, recall and exposure checks

The proposed meaningful-gain gate is **at least three percentage points of mean macro-F1 improvement** over the strongest same-protocol comparator, with a paired whole-game bootstrap 95% lower bound above zero. Require macro precision and macro recall not to regress, derived binary and one-second macro-F1 losses no greater than one point, and supported-class recall losses no greater than five points. These thresholds are proposed research advancement criteria, not a promise or proof of practical reliability. Freeze them before fitting.

Report every class’s true count, predicted count, contributing games, precision, recall and uncertainty. A rare-class improvement claim additionally needs a positive paired recall-difference interval and no precision regression. Proposed evidence sufficiency is representation in at least ten evaluation games and a recall interval no wider than twenty percentage points; insufficient support remains unresolved rather than passing silently. Show seed variation separately from game-sampling uncertainty.

If a compatible later cohort qualifies, seal its latest games before model scoring or model-directed inspection, with a separate calibration block. Score-blind support checks can determine whether the split is evaluable; record amendments before predictions. An unseen 2018 cohort is useful external evidence but cannot demonstrate future-season generalization. Without any qualifying fresh cohort, finish diagnostics and report development gains explicitly; fresh-data acceptance remains unmet.

The final report should include learning curves, ablations, class confusions, calibrated probability quality, and correctness at calibration-selected confidence thresholds targeting 25%, 50% and 75% answer rates, alongside actual evaluation answer rates. Abstentions remain in all-play metrics. Temperature scaling improves confidence estimates, not missing seven-class discrimination. ([Calibration research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html))

Measure training wall time, updates, memory and throughput, plus CPU/GPU inference with preprocessing both included and separated. Keep the earlier batching gains as historical measurements until rebenchmarked on the new workload. The useful outcome is an explanation of which information or data actually improves prediction, with reproducible limits—not another collection of faster experiments that cannot substantiate better coverage predictions.
